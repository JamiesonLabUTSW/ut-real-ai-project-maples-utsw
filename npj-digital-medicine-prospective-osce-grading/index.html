MAPLES in practice: AI‑assisted OSCE assessment

A prospective UTSW study brings multimodal AI into an objective structured clinical examination (OSCE), with targeted human review.

Research update · Oct 16, 2026

Published 7 October 2026 · Article in Press

Prospective deployment of multimodal AI grading for medical student OSCEs

Huong-Tra Ngo and Ameer Hamza Shakur are co-first authors, with Andrew R. Jamieson as the corresponding and senior author.

Part of npj Digital Medicine’s Transforming Medical Education through Artificial Intelligence collection.

Read the paper One-page explainer (PDF)

The study

Assessment across notes, audio and video.

In Fall 2025, MAPLES was deployed prospectively at UT Southwestern in a primarily formative OSCE. Written post-encounter notes, audio and video supported assessment of clinical documentation, communication and physical examination performance.

Second-year medical students
222
OSCE stations per student
5
Retained item-level scores
72,907

A focused role for human review

Low-scoring learners were routed for independent educator rescoring, with physician adjudication of selected AI–human disagreements. The study counted 5,616 actual human grading passes against 72,907 passes in a modeled, single-pass manual comparator: 92.3% fewer grading passes. This comparison counts grading passes; it does not measure time or cost savings.

How to read these findings

Human comparisons focused on selected low-scoring learners at a single institution. They do not establish accuracy across the full student cohort or transfer to other institutions. UT-REAL’s multi-site implementation and validation remain separate work.

02

Governance across deployment cycles

A loop of human oversight

  1. Define the assessment protocol and supporting evidence.

  2. The supervisory committee reviews readiness and proposed changes.

  3. Run the approved workflow and monitor processing and data quality.

  4. Conduct human review, adjudication and quality checks.

  5. Bring operational, agreement and adjudication findings back to the committee.

  6. Use retrospective analysis to inform the next proposed protocol.
